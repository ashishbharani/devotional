<h1>Foreword</h1>
<h2>Dedicated to My Parents</h2>
<p>It began with one small list.</p>
<p>Some journeys begin with a grand vision. This one did not.</p>
<p>It began quietly, with something very small and very personal: a list I was making for myself so that prayers, mantras, hymns, bhajans and other devotional works would be easier to organise and easier to find whenever I needed them.</p>
<p>There was no plan for a book of this scale. There was no thought of a website. I was simply trying to bring a little order to something I valued.</p>
<p>Then I thought of my mother.</p>
<p>Her faith has always been a deeply rooted part of her life. I realised that if this collection could make devotional works easier for me to find, perhaps it could make them easier for her to find as well. From my mother, my thoughts went naturally to my family. And from my family came a question that changed the direction of the project:</p>
<p>Why should it stop with us?</p>
<p>There are prayers people remember only in fragments; hymns once heard at home but no longer easy to locate; traditions inherited from parents and grandparents that younger generations may wish to understand but do not know where to begin. I began to see that the difficulty was not always a lack of devotion. Sometimes it was simply the difficulty of finding what one was looking for.</p>
<p>That was when a personal list began to become something more.</p>
<h2>WHAT IT BECAME</h2>
<p>The purpose was no longer merely to collect. It became an attempt to preserve, organise and make accessible a small part of an extraordinarily vast devotional and cultural inheritance.</p>
<p>Over time, the collection grew far beyond the few works with which it began. It came to include prayers, mantras, stotras, bhajans, kirtans, aartis, sacred texts, saints, temples, festivals, regional traditions, pilgrimage traditions, devotional music and many other expressions of Hindu faith from different parts of India.</p>
<p>Yet I have never wanted the value of this work to be measured only by how many pages it contains or how many titles it catalogues. Numbers can describe the size of a collection. They cannot describe its purpose.</p>
<p>The purpose is connection.</p>
<p>Connection with faith. Connection with memory. Connection with family. Connection with the generations who came before us - and, I hope, with those who will come after us.</p>
<p>If someone rediscovers a prayer remembered from childhood; if a parent finds something meaningful to share with a child; if a younger person becomes curious about a tradition that had begun to feel distant; if an old bhajan unexpectedly brings back the memory of someone who once sang it at home - then the work has already served a purpose that no count of pages can express. FROM THE BOOK TO THE WEBSITE</p>
<p>As the collection grew, it eventually outgrew the idea of being only a book. It found another home on the web.</p>
<p>The Hindu Devotional Collection website is not a separate project. It is another chapter of the same journey - another way of making the collection easier to search, discover, hear, use and share.</p>
<p>https://ashishbharani.github.io/devotional/</p>
<p>Technology has changed the form of the work, but not its reason for existing. A list became a book. A book found a place online. The underlying hope remained the same: that something difficult to find might become easier to reach, and that something worth preserving might become easier to pass on.</p>
<h2>BETWEEN REALISM AND FAITH</h2>
<p>Much of the spirit behind this work comes from the two people to whom I owe far more than words can properly express - my parents.</p>
<p>From my father, I learned the value of hard work, discipline, perseverance and the responsibility of trying to do something properly once one has chosen to undertake it. His outlook has always carried realism and practicality: the understanding that good intentions, however sincere, must eventually be supported by effort.</p>
<p>From my mother came something different, but equally important: faith. Through her, I came to understand the quiet strength that people can draw from devotion - the comfort of prayer in difficult moments, the hope it can offer when answers are not easy to find, and the peace that can come from placing one&#x27;s faith in something greater than oneself.</p>
<p>Somewhere between my father&#x27;s realism and my mother&#x27;s faith, this collection found its direction.</p>
<p>One taught me to keep working. The other taught me why the work might matter.</p>
<p>Perhaps that is why something that began as an exercise in organisation slowly became a labour of love. It also explains why I have found it difficult to call the collection &quot;finished.&quot;</p>
<h2>A LIVING COLLECTION</h2>
<p>Traditions such as ours cannot truly be contained within a final page. They have travelled for centuries through scriptures and temples, saints and scholars, music and festivals, villages and cities, grand ceremonies and quiet household prayers. Most importantly, they have travelled through people: from grandparent to parent, from parent to child, from teacher to disciple, from one home to another. Sometimes through a manuscript. Sometimes through a book. Sometimes through a song. Sometimes through nothing more than a few remembered lines.</p>
<p>I know this collection will never be perfect. There will always be genuine works still waiting to be discovered, traditions deserving better representation, links that change, information that can be improved and errors that should be corrected when found. I would rather acknowledge those limits than pretend that a tradition this vast can ever be completely captured by one compiler or one edition.</p>
<p>So I hope this remains a living collection: capable of growing, capable of being corrected, and capable of making room for the lesser-known as responsibly as for the familiar.</p>
<h2>PASS IT FORWARD</h2>
<p>I do not know how large this collection will eventually become. I do not know whose hands these pages will reach, whose phone will open the website, or whose memories an old prayer may unexpectedly awaken.</p>
<p>I may never know who finds a hymn they had been searching for, which parent shares something from this collection with a son or daughter, or which person comes across a prayer at precisely the moment they needed one.</p>
<p>And perhaps I do not need to know.</p>
<p>Because I know where the journey began.</p>
<p>It began at home.</p>
<p>With the values my father gave me. With the faith I saw in my mother. And with a small list that I once thought I was making only for myself.</p>
<p>If something that began within our family can preserve even a small part of what earlier generations handed to us, and carry it into the hands of generations still to come, then this work will have fulfilled a purpose far greater than the one with which it began.</p>
<h2>TO MY PARENTS</h2>
<p>For the values you gave me, for the discipline that taught me to continue when the work became difficult, for the faith that taught me why such work can have meaning, and for everything you quietly gave me long before this collection existed -</p>
<p>I dedicate this entire endeavour to you, with all my love, respect and gratitude.</p>
<p>These books began because of you. The website grew from that beginning. Whatever this collection may become in the years ahead, a part of both of you will always remain within it.</p>
<p>May something that began within our family find its way into many more. May an old prayer be remembered. May a forgotten tradition be rediscovered. May a parent find something worth passing to a child, and may that child one day pass it further.</p>
<p>And if, somewhere along its journey, this work brings even one person a little closer to faith, family, memory or devotion - that will be blessing enough.</p>
<p>With Pranam, Ashish Bharani Collector &amp; Compiler | Hindu Devotional Collection</p>
